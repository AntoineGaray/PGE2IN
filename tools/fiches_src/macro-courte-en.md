## The exam in 4 lines

- 1h30, in English. **Two subjects to choose from**: one on chapter 1, one on chapter 2.
- **Crossovers allowed** between the two chapters.
- **No introduction or detailed outline required**: a chain of well-argued reasoning, with examples from the course and from everyday life.
- A **touch of humour** is allowed.

## The common thread

**The primacy of institutions** (Rodrik, de Soto): "We get the results that our institutions allow us to get." **Braudel**: "A country has the economic situation of its structures." And the **social contract**: if wealth only benefits a minority, the system will be fought.

## Chapter 1 – The mysteries of development (de Soto)

**The thesis as a chain**: the poor are entrepreneurs → legality costs them too much → their assets have no titles → this is **dead capital**, which can neither circulate nor serve as collateral → they stay poor → the solution is **the law**, open to all, with a **social contract**.

| Key points | |
| --- | --- |
| **De Soto** | Peruvian, born 1941 in Arequipa, liberal. ILD founded in Lima in 1980. *The Other Path* (1986), *The Mystery of Capital* (2000). 4th in the 2021 presidential election (11.6%) |
| **His business in Lima** | 2 sewing machines; 289 days at 6 h a day for 1 employee; $1,230 = 31 minimum wages; 207 operations |
| **Peru** | 728 steps for a housing title in Lima; 90% of small industrial businesses illegal; 85% of urban transport, 60% of food distribution; 13 to 25 years to legalise a plot |
| **Philippines** | Almost 100 steps, 13 to 25 years; 57% of city dwellers and 67% of rural dwellers without a title |
| **Egypt** | 5 to 14 years for a plot; 6 to 12 years to build on agricultural land; more than 90% without a title |
| **Haiti** | 111 steps, 4,112 days for the deed of sale; 19 years in total; 67% of city dwellers and more than 80% of rural dwellers without a title |
| **France** | Permit A38 (Asterix) |
| **Capital** | A reinvested surplus; it is born from the **property title**, which makes assets **fungible** and "extracts an invisible potential" |
| **United States** | 70% of new businesses are financed by a bank loan secured by a mortgage |
| **The causes** | Colonial law (designed to extract), law that protects instead of making capital circulate, conservative lawyers |
| **History** | Colbert's Europe in the 18th century, the conquest of the West in the 19th: more than a century to unify property rights |
| **The debate** | Legalisation is a **necessary but not sufficient condition**: courageous heads of state (Myerson), open-minded lawyers, the population's support, an economic and legal democracy |
| **Results** | 30 to 35 countries advised by the ILD; the 1991 Peruvian law (Ley de Registro de Predios Rurales); inspiration for USAID and the World Bank |
| **Document no. 6** | "The Poor Versus Piketty": r > g ignores the 90% of the world living in the informal economy; Egypt (120 researchers, 47% of "labour" income comes from capital, $360 billion of real estate); Bouazizi, an entrepreneur since the age of 12, 63 suicide attempts, 37 survivors, 4 governments toppled; fictitious capital; Coquelin and revolutionary France |
| **The 4 lessons** | The absence of capital causes poverty; capital and labour are not enemies; the obstacle is the inability to accumulate and protect one's capital; respect for the individual is not specific to the West |
| **The extensions** | Legally fragmented Europe (no real single market); the Arab Springs (small traders without titles); the subprimes ("a financial Third World in the West"); Piketty (2014: he forgets the 90% of the world living in the informal economy) |

**Quotations**: Braudel (capitalism "in a closed system, even encysted": the bell jar); "Poor countries need the solutions that developed countries adopted in the 19th century"; "Democracy cannot be reduced to voting alone"; Polanyi (inequality paves the way for fascism); Samir Amin; T. S. Eliot (the wisdom lost in knowledge); document no. 6: in the West "paper without assets", elsewhere "assets without paper".

## Chapter 2 – The mystery of growth (productivity)

**The thesis as a chain**: is computing a 3rd industrial revolution? → Solow doubts it (1987), then productivity rises in the 2000s → Gordon stays sceptical, Krugman and most economists do not → the real revolution is the **networked organisation of production** (O-ring) → it made **globalisation** and the **emergence of the South** possible → it ends **Fordism** and creates **intra-class inequalities** → a **new social contract** is needed (Rodrik).

| Key points | |
| --- | --- |
| **New economy** | Press: the ICT sector (10 to 15% of US GDP, 5 to 6% of French GDP). Economists: a 3rd industrial revolution, technology + organisation of work |
| **The 2 industrial revolutions** | 1st: England 1760-1830 (steam, railway, loom). 2nd: Europe and the US 1860-1900 (combustion engine, electricity, telephone, running water...). Golden age of productivity: 1913-1972 |
| **Solow (1987)** | "You can see the computer age everywhere but in the productivity statistics." End of the paradox in the 2000s; US: 4% growth a year from 1995 to 2005 |
| **Gordon** | Averaging effect (only the ICT sector progresses), diminishing returns, falling marginal utility, the internet at the office (30 to 40% personal use; Olfeo: 50 min a day), telegraph × 3,000. "The golden age of growth is behind us" (1870-1970). "Gordon and Jesus": waiting for a messiah |
| **Krugman (2013)** | "Indoor plumbing beats iPads", but the androids are coming: speech recognition, translation, self-driving cars. Optimistic about growth, worried about wages |
| **The professor** | Gordon forgets the uses: no Covid vaccine within a year without computers |
| **ICT sector** | 15% of the US economy, 8% of the French. Europe lagging behind; Lisbon 2000. Rent, first-mover advantage, virtuous circle |
| **ICT goods** | Sunk fixed costs, economies of scale, network effects (Metcalfe's law: value = square of the users) → natural monopolies, barriers to entry |
| **US finance** | Capital markets, unicorns (over $1 billion), acquisitions (Microsoft), university-business links. France: insufficient R&D, competitiveness clusters |
| **O-ring** | Michael Kremer, Nobel 2019. A shuttle's seal: one weak link destroys everything. Selective (assortative) matching, fragmentation of production, networked subcontracting |
| **Examples** | Renault Billancourt (Fordist pyramid); Boeing 787, Falcon 7X (designed remotely), Volvo S40 (at least 10 countries) |
| **Inequality** | Small differences in performance, large differences in income (the shuttle engineer versus the Carrefour one). Struggle within classes |
| **Globalisation** | The industrial revolution made globalisation possible, not the reverse. Error → protectionism. Great hope of the 21st century: closing gaps from the top |
| **Maddison** | GDP per capita over 2,000 years, semi-logarithmic scale: everything takes off with the industrial revolutions |
| **Makers** | Chris Anderson (2012), 3D printers, FabLabs, Obama (12/02/2013) |
| **End of Fordism** | Productivity shared with wages (Ford, unions, Keynesians) → end of the post-war social contract → Rodrik: a new social contract |

**The concluding line**: "The fault... is not in our stars, but in ourselves" (Shakespeare, quoted in the opening credits of *Spellbound*, Hitchcock, 1945).

## The 5 crossovers to use

1. **Institutions**: they create capital (chapter 1) and productivity (chapter 2).
2. **The social contract**: threatened by legal exclusion in the South and by the end of Fordism in the North.
3. **Capital that circulates**: de Soto's titles, the finance of US start-ups.
4. **Europe**: legally fragmented (de Soto) and lagging on ICT (Lisbon 2000).
5. **Maddison**: the great divergence comes from technology and institutions.

## Everyday-life examples

A mortgage (living capital) · an endless administrative procedure (A38) · paperwork seen during an internship · the laptop in class (Solow) · personal browsing at the office (Gordon) · the class WhatsApp group (network effects) · group work where one person blocks everything (O-ring) · a smartphone assembled in ten countries (emergence of the South) · two graduates, two salaries (intra-class inequality) · a job for life versus assignments (end of Fordism).

## The reasoning block

**Idea** → **mechanism** ("because... therefore...") → **course example** → **everyday-life example** → **limit or link** to the next idea.
