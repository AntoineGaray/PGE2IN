## The central idea

Attitude, then habits, then analyst. Knowledge grows at each stage. Quantitative thinking is not the same as maths level.

## The 6 steps

1. **Problem recognition**: which problem, why it matters. This step determines the method.
2. **Review of previous findings**: more than half the work.
3. **Modeling (variable selection)**: keep only the useful variables, like a caricature.
4. **Data collection (measurement)**: first check what exists internally, publicly or for purchase.
5. **Data analysis**: compare groups (tests) or measure a dependence (regression).
6. **Results presentation and action**: as much time as the rest (Tufte).

## The habits

- **Demand numbers**: "The plural of anecdote is not data."
- **Never trust numbers**, three doubts: **relevance**, **accuracy**, **correct interpretation**.
- **Correlation is not causation**: only random assignment (test group and control group) proves a cause. The fallacy is called *cum hoc ergo propter hoc*. Example: drinking and cancer, with smoking as the hidden variable.
- **Ask questions**: faced with an average, ask for the standard deviation, outliers, missing data and the median. Faced with a survey, ask who, how, and what wording.
- **Practice quantitative analyses**.

## The 10 questions to ask

Data? Source? Representative sample? Outliers? Assumptions? Conditions that make them invalid? Why this approach? Data transformations? Other approaches rejected? Real causation?

## Think probabilistically

- "Fooled by randomness" (Taleb).
- Birthday paradox: with 23 people, above 50%.
- Stock market = random walk.
- Regression to the mean.
- Probability is the basis of inferential statistics.
