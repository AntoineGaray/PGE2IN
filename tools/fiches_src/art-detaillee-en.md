## China: from the origins to the Han

China defines itself by its culture rather than by a territory: people speak of "5000 years of Civilization" because the borders have changed permanently. Its name, *Zhong Guo* (中国), means "the Middle Kingdom".

### The mythical origins

- **Huang Di**, the Yellow Emperor, the mythical first emperor, supposedly ruling around 2700 BC. The slides present him with **Yan Di** and show the Yellow Emperor's mausoleum.
- The first **Xia dynasty** (around 2000-1600 BC) is considered semi-fictional today.

### Shang Dynasty (1600 – 11th century BC): the writing civilization

- **The oldest proof of Chinese writing**: oracles inscribed on animal bones like turtle shells or ox scapulae.
- This writing **can still be read by contemporary Chinese people**, proof of a very long-lasting common culture.
- The Chinese writing system uses characters, standardized drawings formed by tracing lines in a specific order. Highly pictographic in early history, they became much more abstract.
- **Arts**: bronze objects and carved jade. Art reveals technological improvements (mining, assembling bronze structures) and a large specialized workforce. Literacy grows.

### Zhou Dynasty (around 1050 – 256 BC): the longest dynasty

- Almost **8 centuries** in power.
- **The Mandate of Heaven** (*Tian*): the Zhou justify the overthrow of the Shang by a mandate from heaven, in the name of their self-proclaimed moral superiority. This principle will be the cornerstone of imperial power justification **up until the 20th century**.
- **A unified writing system**: characters are standardized. Supports move from animal bones and bronze to carved wood tablets, bamboo sheets and rolled silk with ink. Literacy grows, but remains restricted to the governing elite.
- **The Warring States period**: imperial power fades, local landlords confront each other. Surprisingly, it is a **golden age for philosophy** and religious thinking.
- **Confucius** (*Kongzi*) tries to define the optimal social and political model. His thought becomes the cornerstone of a kind of **meritocracy** at the base of the Chinese social system up until today.
- **Lao Zi** leads **Taoism**: the world is driven by two antagonist forces, **Yin and Yang** (water and fire, feminine and masculine). The purpose is to follow the *Dao*, the way. Rituals honour ancestors. It is still the biggest locally developed religion in China.
- **Arts**: bronze production at its peak, a new painting style and **lacquers**.

### Qin Dynasty (221 – 206 BC): modeling the Empire

- The Qin State develops faster than the others thanks to its agricultural output (irrigation) and war strategy.
- **Ying Zheng** becomes king at 13, in 246 BC. He grows up in a paranoid environment (assassination attempts, his mother and her lover trying to gain the throne). He relies on a strong military and on external counselors, including Lu Buwei.
- He launches the conquest of all rival states at 22, with an army of about a million soldiers. In **221 BC** he submits the last resisting state (Qi) and becomes **Qin Shi Huang Di**, "the first emperor". Capital: **Xianyang** (today a district of Xi'an). He conquers the south up to part of present-day Vietnam.
- **The political legacy** ("foundations for 2000 years of imperial ruling"): an extremely centralized and efficient administration (new infrastructures, the **Great Wall**), meritocracy and the **legalist school**, and the **standardization of writing** (the Qin script imposed on all territories), which also boosts economic exchanges.
- **The quest for immortality**: a failure, but it leaves the **Terra Cotta Warriors Army**, discovered in the 20th century next to Xi'an.
- A very short-lived dynasty: after his death, counselors fight over power and the Han take the throne.

### Han Dynasty (202 BC – 220 AD): at the heart of Chinese culture and expansion

- About **4 centuries**. The founder, **Liu Bang** (died 195 BC), keeps the Qin's centralized administration.
- **The Xiongnu**, a federation of semi-nomadic tribes from the north, defeat the Han (198 BC according to the course) and impose heavy tributes. Their raids continue anyway.
- **Emperor Wu** (141-87 BC) launches massive military campaigns, takes the **Hexi corridor** and expands towards Central Asia.
- **The Silk Road**: "a first example of international trade going global with China at its center".
- Under Wu, **Confucianism becomes the only state-supported ideology**, until the fall of the Qing. The professor's summary says "beginning of the 19th century", but the Qing fell in 1912, i.e. at the beginning of the **20th century**.
- Poetry and literature flourish, with historical records that impose the Han vision of China's history.
- **A refined culture**: silk is also worn in China (drinking tea with friends, music, poetry, astronomy). The slides show tomb paintings, pottery, silk and tea sets.
- **The Han identity**: characters are called *Hanzi*, the language *Hanyu*, traditional clothes and way of life *Hanfu*. **Hanfu** is becoming trendy again in modern China.
- New styles in painting, calligraphy, sculpture and pottery. Through the Silk Road, **Buddhism enters China** and spreads across East Asia.

## China: from the Six Dynasties to the Ming

Chinese history follows a cycle: a unified, brilliant empire, a decline, a division, then reunification by force. The Sui repeat the Qin; the Tang repeat the Han.

### From the fall of the Han to the Song

| Period | Dates | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Six Dynasties (division) | 220 – 589 | The Han collapse in 220 AD, China is divided |
| **Sui** | 581 – 618 (less than 40 years) | Swift reunification through military campaigns, like the Qin. The **Grand Canal** |
| **Tang** | 618 – 907 (about 3 centuries) | Another golden age for the arts (painting, sculpture). The Silk Road modernized and secured. Decline due mostly to emperors' disinterest in ruling |
| **Song** | 960 – 1279 | **Printing** and **theatre**. The Song are defeated by the Mongols |

The dates of the Six Dynasties, the start of the Sui and the end of the Tang (220-589, 581, 907) are standard additions: the professor's summary does not give them.

### Yuan Dynasty (1279 – 1368): China under the Mongols

- **Kublai**, grandson of Genghis Khan, defeats the Song in 1279. The Mongols had already seized northern China decades earlier. It is the **first non-Han ethnic dynasty**.
- The Mongol empire stretches from the China Sea to Europe, which secures trade along the Silk Road and by sea. China enters a new phase of modernity.
- **Marco Polo** visits China and becomes a counselor and ambassador of Kublai.
- Decline after Kublai's death (1291), then overthrow by the Ming in 1368.
- **Arts**: under the Tang and Song, art was controlled and encouraged by the state. Under the Yuan, painters **isolate themselves from power** because they refuse to work for the invaders.
- **White and blue ceramic** is introduced. It will become a worldwide success under the Ming.
- **Literature**: fictional drama novels quickly gain huge success. They pave the way for the great classics such as *Journey to the West*.

### Ming Dynasty (1368 – 1644): the last Han rulers

- A rebellion pushes the Yuan back to the Mongolian steppes. In 1368, its leader seizes Beijing and becomes **Hongwu**, first Ming emperor.
- **Hongwu** (about 30 years of reign, died 1398) avoids directly challenging the Yuan legacy. He builds diplomatic links with Korea, Japan and Tibet, and extends the empire, especially in the north and north-east.
- **Yongle** (Zhu Di, emperor from 1402) opens China to the world.
    - He moves the capital to **Beijing** and builds the **Forbidden City** there, "a pharaonic project".
    - He builds and reinforces the **Great Wall** as we know it today.
    - He launches the expeditions of his eunuch servant **Zheng He** and his fleet to South-East Asia and East Africa.
- By the 16th century, the Ming offer commercial counters to the Spanish, Portuguese and Dutch. **Ceramics** are one of China's bestsellers abroad, especially in Europe (a class presentation topic).
- **The decline**:
    - Under Emperor **Wanli** (late 16th century), **economic dependency on imported silver** (from South America via European countries, and from Japan) creates hyperinflation.
    - Natural disasters and famines in the early 17th century.
    - The rise of the **Manchus**, who depose the Ming and found the Qing.
- **Art and culture**: novel and drama at their peak (*Journey to the West*), and white and blue ceramic.

### Addition for the Ming presentation (not in the professor's summary)

Blue and white porcelain is decorated with **cobalt blue** (originally imported from Persia) under a transparent glaze. It is mass-produced in the imperial kilns of **Jingdezhen**. It is exported to Europe by the Portuguese, then by the Dutch East India Company ("kraak" porcelain). It will be copied in Europe, for example in Delft.

## China: from the Qing to today

The Qing take China to its greatest size, but miss modernization. Then come the Republic, civil war, Mao and the Cultural Revolution, then the opening. Since 2013, Xi Jinping has brought control back.

### Qing Dynasty (1644 – 1912): the last dynasty

- **The Manchu takeover**: the north-eastern peoples, integrated at the borders by the Ming, unite under the ethnic name of **Manchu** as Ming power fades. They seize Beijing and the Forbidden City in **1644**.
- **Shunzhi** (rules until 1661) is the first Manchu emperor. He completes the conquest of the remaining Ming strongholds in the south.
- **Kangxi** (60 years of reign) mixes Han and Manchu officials to avoid discontentment, keeping Manchus in leading positions. He **bans Christian priests** from the West to minimize foreign cultural influences.
- **Qianlong** (1735-1796), the apex: the **10 great campaigns** give China a size even bigger than contemporary China. With peace and new crops (potatoes, peanuts), the population reaches **400 million** by his death.
- **Economic isolationism**: to avoid the dependency that brought down the Ming, trade with the West is strictly forbidden, apart from controlled zones such as **Canton** (Guangzhou). The trade imbalance with Europe enrages Great Britain.
- **The Opium Wars** (**1839-1842** and **1856-1860**): the British want to sell their opium; the Qing refuse because of the drug's dramatic impact on the population.
- **Empress dowager Cixi**: China enters the 20th century as a fading and isolated superpower, behind in political, technological and economic terms.
- **The fall in 1912**, after the **Xinhai revolution** and the proclamation of the Republic of China.
- **Arts**: **Peking Opera**, a Qing legacy still alive (session 4 presentation).

### Short-lived republic and communist revolution

- **Sun Yat-sen**, President of the Republic of China (ROC), tries to build a parliamentary system. The nationalist forces in power face the growing communist movement led by **Mao Zedong**.
- The civil war is paused in 1936 to face **Japan**, which invaded **Manchuria in 1931**. It restarts after 1945.
- **1949**: victory of the communists; the nationalists retreat to **Taiwan**. China has been under **CCP** rule since.
- **Mao** leads for almost 25 years: authoritarian power and massive cultural destructions.
- **The Cultural Revolution** is the key period for art. The slides give its context and impact with no readable text. As a standard addition: 1966-1976, the Red Guards, the campaign against the "Four Olds" (old ideas, culture, customs and habits), the destruction of temples and works, and art reduced to propaganda (posters, model operas).

### China today

- After Mao's death, the **opening and reforms** period brings stunning economic growth and increased relative freedom, **up until 2013 and Xi Jinping**.
- **Re-appropriation of the old Chinese culture**, with a strong twist of nationalism (for example the Hanfu trend).
- **A flourishing contemporary art scene**, mixing modernity, old style and light social criticism. Example quoted in the slides: **Yue Minjun** (his self-portraits with a frozen laugh).
- **Soft power** is rising, with science-fiction literature and the film industry.
- **Strong control and the isolationism temptation** are back under Xi Jinping.

## Japan: from the Jomon to today

Japan alternates long closures and short but decisive openings: China and Buddhism, the Europeans in the 16th century, then the forced opening of 1853. Its historical development is radically different from China's.

### The main periods

| Period | Dates (course) | Key points | Art |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Jomon** | From around 10,000 BC | Indigenous semi-nomadic inhabitants, isolated on the island, without organized agriculture or forging, hence no urban centres or structured social system | Pottery made with **cord** and clay (*jomon* means "cord-marked") |
| **Yayoi** | 800 BC – 250 AD | Migrants from mainland Asia (around 1000 BC) who mix with the Jomon. Forging, mining, weapons, agricultural tools. Around 4 million inhabitants by the end. Permanent settlements, then multiple small kingdoms | Ornamental **bronzes** inspired by mainland productions, pottery |
| **Unification under imperial rule (Yamato)** | 250 – 538 | The **Yamato** imperial house, the longest imperial lineage in the world, still ruling today. Recognized by China, hence transfers of techniques and ideas | — |
| **Asuka** | 538 – 794 | **The introduction of Buddhism**, a massive increase in literacy using Chinese ideograms. A mix of **Shinto** (the ancestral religion) and Buddhism | Buddhist painting, sculpture and architecture |
| **Heian** | 794 – 1185 | **A peak in art and culture**. The capital moves to **Kyoto**, where it stays until 1868. The court acquires artistic artifacts of all kinds | Architecture mixing Chinese and Indian influences. The **Emaki** (picture scroll illustrating famous novels and historical events). By the 12th century it includes short texts and dialogues: the **ancestor of manga** |
| **Feudal Japan (shogunate and samurai)** | 1185 – 1600 | A military administration (the shogun), fights between local warlords and their **Samurai**. Nevertheless strong economic, cultural and artistic development | A new ink style, **Himeji Castle** (1609) |
| Opening to the rest of the world | 1543 – 1635 | A short opening to the Europeans | — |
| **Edo** | 1600 – 1868 | The **Tokugawa** shogunate, based in Edo (Tokyo). **Total closure** to foreign influences from the 1630s to the 1850s, very strict control of the population. Stability brings strong economic development | A rich merchant class invests in the arts. **Woodblock printing** spreads painting widely (the slides show prints from 1680, 1794 and 1830). Strong refinement in ceramics and lacquerware |
| **Meiji** | 1868 – 1912 | End of the shogunate, the emperor returns. **Full-scale modernization**, especially technology and industrialization | Foreign influences, Western advisers |

The Edo paradox must be explained: closing to the world was **a booster** for local artists, because they were supported by the newly rich merchants and woodblock printing made works affordable.

As an addition, not in the course text: these prints are **ukiyo-e**. The 1830 print is very probably Hokusai's *The Great Wave off Kanagawa* (around 1830-1831), but the slides do not confirm it.

### The end of Edo and Meiji

- The Edo economy declines from the late 18th century.
- In **1853**, a US military fleet destroys Japan's defences and imposes a forced trade opening. This is Commodore Perry, a standard addition.
- The humiliated Tokugawa shogunate surrenders power to the emperor and his allies. This starts the **Meiji Era**, which fully shaped modern Japan.

### Japan under US control

- Strong political, economic and cultural influences from the US, including **pacifism** written into the Constitution.
- **Manga culture** (presentation "Manga Culture and US occupation after WWII").
- **Soft power**, with Nintendo for example.
- **Economic leadership in Asia**, up until the fast rise of China, then a slow technological decline.

## Korea and India

The slides on these two countries contain only titles. The details below come from standard knowledge and follow the professor's outline. They should be checked against the session 7 notes.

### Korea: growing between China and Japan

The professor's outline: growing between China and Japan, Japanese occupation and the Korean War, modern South Korea. The syllabus question: how did it become one of the most influential countries in global entertainment?

| Period | Dates (slides) | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Goryeo** | 918 – 1392 | Gives its name to "Korea". Buddhism is the state religion. Celadons (inlaid grey-green ceramics). The *Tripitaka Koreana* carved on woodblocks |
| **Joseon** | 1392 – 1897 | Neo-Confucianism replaces Buddhism. King Sejong creates the **Hangeul** alphabet (1443-1446). White porcelain |
| Buddhist influences and syncretism | — | Art mixing Chinese contributions, Buddhism and local traditions |
| **Under Japanese rule** | 1910 – 1945 | Colonization, forced cultural assimilation |
| **Korean War** | 1950 – 1953 | Division of the country into North and South |
| **Modern South Korea** | — | The soft power of the **Hallyu** (Korean wave): *Gangnam Style* (PSY, 2012, the first video to reach a billion views on YouTube), **K-pop**, cinema (presentation "The rise of Korean movies industry", for example *Parasite*, Palme d'Or 2019 and Oscar 2020) |

### India: a cultural and artistic giant

The professor's outline: an ancient civilization with a global impact, spirituality shaping empires, British colonization.

| Period | Key points |
| --- | --- |
| **Indus Valley Civilization** (around 2600-1900 BC) | Planned cities (Harappa, Mohenjo-daro), carved seals, an undeciphered script |
| **Vedic period** | The **Vedas**, sacred texts in Sanskrit, the roots of Hinduism. **Buddhism** is born in India with the Buddha (Siddhartha Gautama, around the 5th century BC) |
| **Maurya Empire** (4th-2nd century BC) | **Ashoka** converts to Buddhism and spreads it across Asia (pillars and edicts, the Sanchi stupa) |
| **Delhi Sultanate**, then the Mughal Empire | The slides place the **Taj Mahal** in this part. In fact it is **Mughal**: built by Shah Jahan for his wife Mumtaz Mahal in the 17th century, it mixes Persian, Islamic and Indian styles |
| **British East India Company** (17th century) | Trading posts, then progressive control of the territory |
| **British Raj** (1858 – 1947) | The British Crown rules India directly |
| **Independence with Gandhi** (1947) | Non-violence and civil disobedience (Salt March, 1930), then the India-Pakistan partition |

## How to answer an essay question

The syllabus assesses the ability to link art and culture to the economy and business. For each answer:

1. Place it in time (dynasty and dates).
2. Give a precise work or practice (Shang bronze, the Terra Cotta Army, blue and white ceramic, the Emaki, ukiyo-e, K-pop).
3. Explain the link with power, religion or the economy (the Mandate of Heaven, the Silk Road, dependency on silver, soft power).
4. End with a contemporary echo (the Hanfu trend, manga, the Hallyu, McDonald's in India).
