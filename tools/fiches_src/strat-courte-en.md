## The 4 great authors

| Author | Date | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Andrews** (Harvard) | 1971 | *The Concept of Corporate Strategy*: mission, objectives, policies. LCAG model (ancestor of SWOT). Defining, formulating, implementing strategy |
| **Ansoff** | 1965 | *Corporate Strategy*: strategic planning, product/market matrix, synergy |
| **Porter** | 1980, 1985, 1990 | 5 forces, value chain, diamond. Strategy = objectives + means |
| **Mintzberg** | — | Deliberate + emergent = realized strategy. The 5 Ps. The 3 fallacies of strategic planning. The 10 schools (*Strategy Safari*) |

- **5 Ps**: Plan, Ploy, Pattern, Position, Perspective.
- **3 fallacies of strategic planning**: predetermination, detachment, formalization.
- **Origins**: Sun Tzu (*The Art of War*), *strategos* (the general), game theory, then the competitive environment (technology, globalization, demanding consumers).

## The 5 + 1 forces

1. Rivalry between competitors (number, similarity, fixed costs, exit barriers).
2. Bargaining power of customers and distributors (concentration, low switching costs).
3. Bargaining power of suppliers (concentration, differentiation, switching costs).
4. Threat of substitutes (technology, value for money, they come from another industry).
5. Threat of new entrants (barriers to entry: scale, distribution, retaliation, laws, brand).
6. **+1: the State** (taxes, laws, subsidies, diplomacy), added after Porter.

## PESTEL and scenarios

- **PESTEL**: Political, Economic, Socio-cultural, Technological, Environmental, Legal. Look at the combination of trends, identify the **key drivers for change**, and it prepares the **SWOT** (opportunities and threats).
- **Scenarios in 4 steps**: key variables, guesses (optimistic, probable, pessimistic), scenarios, a strategy for each scenario.
- **Environment**: turbulence (Emery and Trist, 4 types), complexity, hostility. Desreumaux matches a type of company to each environment.
- **Competitive advantage**: identifiable by consumers, sustainable, defensible, non-substitutable.

## Definitions

- **Strategy**: allocating resources for the long term and shaping the organization's scope to gain a competitive edge, adapting resources and skills to changing conditions and to stakeholders' expectations.
- **Strategic management** (David, 2011): the art and science of formulating, implementing and evaluating cross-functional decisions. It is the function of general management, the captain of the boat.
- **The 3 stages**: formulation (vision, SWOT, objectives, choice), implementation (annual objectives, resources, culture, structure), evaluation (measure, correct).
- **Vision, mission, goals, objectives (quantifiable), resources, results.**
- **Industry** (companies with similar products), **sector** (the term used for public services), **market** (customers with a common need).
- **Industry structures**: monopolistic, oligopolistic, hypercompetitive, perfectly competitive.
- **Strategic group**: the companies in a sector with similar strategies.
- **KSF**: what it takes to succeed in a segment. **Strategic segment**: same skills and same KSFs, with intra-segment homogeneity and inter-segment heterogeneity. Ansoff (product-market, 1965) and Abell and Hammond (+ the function, 1979).

## Contexts

- **SME**: few markets, influential founders, undercapitalisation that limits strategic options.
- **Multinational**: parent company–subsidiary relations, resource allocation between strategic business areas, logistics.
- **Public sector**: ideology, government control, competition to obtain resources.
- **Services**: advantage rests on intangibles ("The future of industry lies in services").

## Examples to quote

- **Free Mobile**: a new entrant breaks the business model (low prices, no stores, no network), 13% of the market, a market created in volume but destroyed in value (−33% average revenue per subscriber).
- **Renault contract 2009**: vision ("Europe's most profitable generalist carmaker"), objectives (6% margin, +800,000 sales), resources (26 models, Logan).
- **Apple, Uber, TSMC (2025)**: vertical integration, alliances or protection.

## Case structure

1. The problem in one sentence.
2. The external diagnosis (PESTEL, then 5 + 1 forces).
3. The internal diagnosis (strengths, weaknesses, business model).
4. The options, with their advantages and risks.
5. **The justified recommendation**, with its risks.
