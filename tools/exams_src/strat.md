@intro
**Introduction to Strategic Management – Mock final exam – 1h30 – 20 points.** No documents allowed. Part 1 tests the course; Parts 2 to 4 are a single case study on Velora. Quote authors and models by name, and finish every case answer with a clear recommendation.

**Velora** is a French maker of electric cargo bikes, founded in Lyon in 2015. It has a turnover of **85 M€** and **420 employees**, and assembles its bikes in France. 60 % of sales come from families (B2C) and 40 % from delivery companies (B2B). Its three largest B2B clients represent 25 % of turnover.

- **Suppliers**: batteries and motors come from two Asian suppliers; one Chinese supplier provides 70 % of the battery cells. In 2024, a shipping crisis delayed deliveries by three months.
- **Market**: French e-bike sales grew by about 20 % a year until 2023, then fell by 8 % in 2025.
- **Competitors**: more than 30 brands. A large sports retailer sells an entry-level cargo bike at half of Velora's price, and a European carmaker has announced its own range for 2027.
- **Substitutes**: e-scooters, small electric vans, public transport.
- **Public policy**: the French purchase bonus for cargo bikes fell from 2,000 € to 500 € in 2025; more cities are creating low-emission zones that ban old diesel vans; the EU applies anti-dumping duties on Chinese e-bikes; a new EU regulation will require a "battery passport" (traceability) from 2027.
- **Society and technology**: more urban families cycle to work, bike theft is a major worry, connected bikes and new battery technologies are emerging.

The CEO hesitates between three options for 2027-2030:

- **Option A**: build a battery assembly plant in France with a partner (40 M€ investment).
- **Option B**: launch the current range in Germany and the Benelux.
- **Option C**: launch a leasing offer for delivery companies (bike, maintenance, insurance and anti-theft tracking for a monthly fee).
@method
**Course questions:** define, explain, then illustrate with an author or an example from the course.

**Case study:** follow the course framework: problem in one sentence, external diagnosis (PESTEL limited to the key drivers, then the 5 + 1 forces), options compared in a table, then **one** recommendation with its risks and a condition for success. Listing tools without concluding loses points.

@part 1 | Course | Part 1 – Course questions | 6 | 25
@q 1.1 | 2
Explain Mintzberg's distinction between deliberate and emergent strategy, and present his 5 Ps of strategy.
@c
**Model answer:**

For Henry Mintzberg (*The Rise and Fall of Strategic Planning*, *Strategy Safari*), strategy is not only planned in advance. A **deliberate strategy** is the intended strategy that is actually carried out. An **emergent strategy** grows out of day-to-day decisions and adaptations that were never planned. The **realised strategy** mixes both, and part of the intended strategy is never realised.

His **5 Ps** show that strategy has several meanings: a **Plan** (a deliberate course of action), a **Ploy** (a manoeuvre to outwit a competitor), a **Pattern** (consistency of behaviour over time), a **Position** (the place in the market) and a **Perspective** (the organisation's worldview and culture).

This view criticises formal planning, which makes three errors: predetermination, detachment from operations and formalisation.

**Checklist (2 pts):**

- ☐ Deliberate vs emergent, and the realised strategy mixes both (0.5)
- ☐ The 5 Ps named (0.5)
- ☐ Each P briefly explained (0.5)
- ☐ Mintzberg's book or the 3 errors of planning cited (0.5)
@q 1.2 | 2
Present the Ansoff matrix. For each of the four strategies, give its logic and its level of risk.
@c
**Model answer:**

Igor Ansoff (*Corporate Strategy*, 1965) crosses **products** (existing or new) with **markets** (existing or new) to classify growth strategies:

| | Existing products | New products |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Existing markets** | **Market penetration**: win market share (aggressive marketing, promotions). Lowest risk | **Product development**: innovate for current customers. Medium risk |
| **New markets** | **Market development**: new regions or new targets. Medium risk | **Diversification**: new products on new markets. Highest risk |

Risk rises as the company moves away from what it knows: diversification combines two unknowns. The model was later criticised because it favours commercial know-how over technological know-how.

**Checklist (2 pts):** the two axes (0.5); the four strategies named correctly (0.5); the logic of each (0.5); the risk ranking, with diversification as the riskiest (0.5).
@q 1.3 | 2
What is strategic management according to David (2011)? Describe its three stages.
@c
**Model answer:**

Strategic management is the function of **general management**, not the sum of the operational functions (marketing, finance, production, HR). For David (2011), it is the art and science of formulating, implementing and evaluating cross-functional decisions that enable an organisation to achieve its objectives. It deals with ambiguous, non-routine situations at the level of the whole organisation.

1. **Formulation**: define the vision and mission, identify external opportunities and threats, assess internal strengths and weaknesses, set long-term objectives, generate alternatives and choose strategies.
2. **Implementation**: set annual objectives, define policies, motivate employees, allocate resources, adapt the structure and culture, prepare budgets, link pay to performance.
3. **Evaluation**: review internal and external factors, measure performance, take corrective action.

The course image: without general management, the company is a crew without a captain, or a crew where everyone thinks they are the captain.

**Checklist (2 pts):** role of general management (0.5); David's definition (0.5); the three stages named (0.5); at least two actions per stage (0.5).

@q 2.2 | 1.5
Build three scenarios for Velora's market in 2030 (optimistic, probable, pessimistic) based on two key variables, with a strategic response for each.
@c
Key variables: **spread of low-emission zones** and **level of public support for cargo bikes**.

| Scenario | Hypotheses | Strategic response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Optimistic | Low-emission zones in all large cities, support restored | Increase B2B capacity, expand abroad |
| Probable | Low-emission zones spread slowly, little public support | Focus on B2B, where demand is driven by regulation rather than subsidies |
| Pessimistic | Low-emission zones delayed, no support, carmaker's range arrives in 2027 | Cut costs, protect the premium niche, secure supply |

@q 2.3 | 1.5
How would you characterise Velora's environment using the course criteria? Which type of environment is it according to Emery and Trist?
@c
- **Turbulence: high.** Demand swung from +20 % to −8 %, public policy changes quickly, and a carmaker is about to enter.
- **Complexity: high.** Many interacting factors: technology, EU regulation, supply chain, consumer habits.
- **Hostility: high.** More than 30 competitors, a low-cost retailer, dependence on concentrated suppliers.

According to Emery and Trist (1965), this is a **type IV environment**, complex and turbulent: Velora must monitor competitors, the economy and the market at the same time. Desreumaux would add that a turbulent, complex environment calls for a flexible, decentralised organisation.

**Checklist (1.5 pt):** the three criteria assessed with facts (0.75); Emery and Trist type IV (0.5); one consequence for the organisation (0.25).

@q 3.1 | 3
Analyse the attractiveness of the cargo-bike industry with Porter's 5 (+1) forces. Rate each force (low, medium, high).
@c
| Force | Rating | Justification from the case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competitive rivalry | High | More than 30 brands, falling demand, a low-cost competitor at half price |
| Bargaining power of suppliers | High | One Chinese supplier provides 70 % of cells; few alternatives; 2024 delays |
| Bargaining power of buyers | Medium to high | Three B2B clients = 25 % of sales; families are price-sensitive |
| Threat of substitutes | Medium | E-scooters, small electric vans, public transport |
| Threat of new entrants | High | A European carmaker enters in 2027 with brand and capital |
| State (+1) | Ambivalent | Bonus cut (negative), low-emission zones and anti-dumping duties (positive), battery passport (constraint) |

**Conclusion:** the industry is **not very attractive** in the long run for a mid-sized player: profitability is squeezed by rivalry, suppliers and new entrants. Velora must find an advantage that customers can identify and that is durable, defensible and hard to copy.

**Checklist (3 pts):** 0.4 per force rated and justified with a fact (2.4); overall conclusion on attractiveness (0.6).
@q 3.2 | 1
Give two key success factors (KSF) for the B2B segment and explain why B2B and B2C can be considered two different strategic segments.
@c
- **KSF for B2B:** reliability and uptime of the bikes (maintenance), total cost of use over time, after-sales service and fleet management. Other accepted answers: load capacity, delivery lead times.
- **Two strategic segments:** a segment groups activities that share the same KSF. Families buy on price, design and subsidies; delivery companies buy on reliability, service and total cost. The KSF are homogeneous within each segment and different between them (intra-segment homogeneity, inter-segment heterogeneity), so they are two strategic segments.

**Checklist (1 pt):** two relevant B2B KSF (0.5); the definition of a strategic segment applied (0.5).

@c

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Battery plant | Outside the Ansoff matrix: backward vertical integration | Reduces supplier power, eases the battery passport, secures supply | 40 M€, about half of turnover; new skills; slow return |

@q 4.2 | 2
Which option do you recommend? Justify with your diagnosis, give the main risk and a condition for success.
@c
**Model answer:**

**I recommend option C, the leasing offer for delivery companies.**

The diagnosis shows that the key driver of demand is now the spread of low-emission zones, which affects B2B customers, while B2C demand suffers from the end of the bonus. Option C targets the growing segment and changes Velora's business model: a recurring revenue stream, a service that a low-cost retailer cannot easily copy, and long contracts that reduce the bargaining power of the three large clients. As the course puts it, "the future of industry lies in services". It is also a product development strategy in Ansoff's matrix, so less risky than diversification.

Option A would reduce supplier power, but 40 M€ is about half of Velora's turnover, which is too heavy in a falling market. Option B keeps the supplier dependence and puts Velora against strong local brands.

**Main risk:** financing the fleet of leased bikes (working capital) and keeping uptime high. **Condition for success:** a partner bank or leasing company to finance the fleet, and a maintenance network in the main cities. In the medium term, Velora can secure a second battery supplier to reduce its dependence without building a plant.

**Checklist (2 pts):** one clear option (0.5); justified by at least two facts from the diagnosis (0.5); why the other options are rejected (0.5); risk and condition for success (0.5).
