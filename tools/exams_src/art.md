@intro
**Art and Culture in Asia – Mock final exam – 1h30 – 20 points.** No documents or calculator allowed. Part 1 is a multiple-choice quiz on dates, dynasties, people and works (one correct answer per question). Part 2 asks for short explanations. Part 3 is an essay.
@method
**Multiple choice:** the distractors are often real dynasties or dates from another period; place each answer on the timeline before choosing.

**Short explanations:** give the dynasty or period **and** a date, then one precise detail.

**Essay:** the course links art and culture to power, religion and the economy. For each argument: place it in time (dynasty and dates), give a precise work or practice, explain the link with power or trade, and end with a contemporary echo.

@part 1 | MCQ | Part 1 – Multiple-choice questions | 6 | 20
@q 1.1 | 0.5
Who became the "First Emperor" of China in 221 BC?

A. Liu Bang · B. Emperor Wu · C. Ying Zheng (Qin Shi Huang Di) · D. Kublai Khan
@c
**C. Ying Zheng**, who took the name **Qin Shi Huang Di** after conquering the last rival state (Qi). Capital: Xianyang, near today's Xi'an. Liu Bang founded the Han, Emperor Wu was a Han emperor, Kublai Khan founded the Yuan.
@q 1.2 | 0.5
Which dynasty introduced the Mandate of Heaven to justify its power?

A. Shang · B. Zhou · C. Qin · D. Han
@c
**B. The Zhou** (c. 1050–256 BC), to justify the overthrow of the Shang in the name of moral superiority. The principle legitimised imperial power until the 20th century.
@q 1.3 | 0.5
Under which emperor did Confucianism become the single official ideology?

A. Qin Shi Huang Di · B. Yongle · C. Qianlong · D. Emperor Wu of the Han
@c
**D. Emperor Wu of the Han** (141–87 BC). It remained the official ideology until the fall of the Qing in 1912. Qin Shi Huang Di relied on Legalism instead.
@q 1.4 | 0.5
What is the oldest trace of Chinese writing?

A. Silk scrolls of the Han · B. Oracle inscriptions on turtle shells and ox bones under the Shang · C. Bamboo strips of the Zhou · D. Woodblock prints of the Song
@c
**B. Oracle inscriptions under the Shang** (1600–11th century BC). This writing can still be read by Chinese people today. Bamboo strips and silk came later, under the Zhou.
@q 1.5 | 0.5
Which two innovations are associated with the Song dynasty (960–1279)?

A. Printing and theatre · B. The Great Wall and the Grand Canal · C. Lacquer and bronze · D. Porcelain and the Forbidden City
@c
**A. Printing and theatre.** The Grand Canal is Sui, the Great Wall Qin (rebuilt by the Ming), bronze Shang and Zhou, the Forbidden City Ming. The Song were defeated by the Mongols in 1279.
@q 1.6 | 0.5
Blue-and-white porcelain appeared under the … and became a worldwide success under the …

A. Tang / Song · B. Song / Yuan · C. Ming / Qing · D. Yuan / Ming
@c
**D. Yuan / Ming.** Under the Ming, it was one of China's best-selling products in Europe.
@q 1.7 | 0.5
Which Ming emperor moved the capital to Beijing, built the Forbidden City and launched Zheng He's voyages?

A. Yongle · B. Hongwu · C. Wanli · D. Kangxi
@c
**A. Yongle** (emperor from 1402). He also rebuilt the Great Wall as we know it today. Hongwu founded the Ming, Wanli reigned during its decline, Kangxi was a Qing emperor.
@q 1.8 | 0.5
When did the two Opium Wars take place?

A. 1644 and 1661 · B. 1894 and 1904–1905 · C. 1911 and 1937 · D. 1839–1842 and 1856–1860
@c
**D. 1839–1842 and 1856–1860.** Britain wanted to sell its opium; the Qing refused because of the damage caused by the drug. 1644 is the Manchu conquest, 1894 the first Sino-Japanese war.
@q 1.9 | 0.5
When did the last Chinese dynasty fall?

A. 1644 · B. 1912 · C. 1842 · D. 1949
@c
**B. 1912**, after the **Xinhai Revolution** and the proclamation of the Republic of China (Sun Yat-sen). 1949 is the Communist victory.
@q 1.10 | 0.5
The emaki, an illustrated handscroll considered the ancestor of manga, is typical of which Japanese period?

A. Jomon · B. Edo · C. Heian · D. Meiji
@c
**C. Heian** (794–1185), the peak of art and culture, with Kyoto as capital. In the 12th century, emaki began to combine images with text and dialogue.
@q 1.11 | 0.5
What happened in Japan in 1853?

A. The capital moved to Kyoto · B. The Tokugawa shogunate was founded · C. An American fleet forced Japan to open to trade · D. Japan invaded Manchuria
@c
**C. An American fleet forced Japan to open** (Commodore Perry). The Tokugawa returned power to the emperor: this began the **Meiji era**. Manchuria was invaded in 1931.
@q 1.12 | 0.5
What is the Hallyu?

A. The Korean Wave, the global spread of South Korean pop culture · B. A Japanese school of painting · C. A Chinese dynasty · D. An Indian religious text
@c
**A. The Korean Wave**, a major source of soft power: K-pop, PSY's *Gangnam Style* (2012, first YouTube video to reach one billion views), the film *Parasite* (Palme d'Or 2019, Oscar 2020).

@part 2 | Explain | Part 2 – Short explanations | 6 | 25
@q 2.1 | 1.5
Explain the "paradox of Edo" (1600–1868).
@c
**Model answer:**

Under the Tokugawa shogunate, based in Edo (Tokyo), Japan was almost **completely closed** to the outside world. Paradoxically, this isolation **boosted local art**: merchants grew rich in a long period of peace and supported artists, and **woodblock printing** made works cheap and accessible to a wide urban public. This gave rise to the woodblock prints known as ukiyo-e.

**Checklist (1.5 pt):** isolation under the Tokugawa with dates (0.5); merchants' wealth as patrons (0.5); woodblock printing making art accessible (0.5).
@q 2.2 | 1.5
The course describes a cycle in Chinese history. Explain it with two examples.
@c
**Model answer:**

Chinese history follows a cycle: a **unified and brilliant empire**, then **decline**, **division**, and **reunification by force**. The short, brutal dynasty that reunifies the country is followed by a long, brilliant one.

- The **Qin** (221–206 BC) unified China by force and lasted only 15 years; the **Han** (202 BC–220 AD) kept their centralised administration and ruled for about four centuries.
- After the division of the Six Dynasties (220–589), the **Sui** (581–618) reunified the country by force in less than 40 years; the **Tang** (618–907) brought a new golden age of the arts.

**Checklist (1.5 pt):** the four stages of the cycle (0.5); Qin then Han (0.5); Sui then Tang (0.5).
@q 2.3 | 1.5
Why did the Ming dynasty decline, and how did the Qing try to avoid the same fate? What was the consequence?
@c
**Model answer:**

Under Emperor **Wanli** (late 16th century), the Ming became **dependent on imported silver** (from South America through the Europeans, and from Japan), which caused hyperinflation. Natural disasters and famines followed, and the **Manchus** overthrew the Ming in **1644**.

To avoid this dependence, the Qing chose **economic isolationism**: trade with the West was banned except in controlled zones such as **Canton** (Guangzhou). The resulting trade imbalance angered Britain and led to the **Opium Wars** (1839–1842 and 1856–1860), which left China weakened and behind.

**Checklist (1.5 pt):** silver dependence and Wanli (0.5); Qing isolationism and Canton (0.5); the Opium Wars as a consequence (0.5).
@q 2.4 | 1.5
Compare Confucianism and Taoism: founder, main idea, and influence on Chinese society.
@c
| | Confucianism | Taoism |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Founder | **Confucius** (Kongzi), late Zhou period | **Lao Zi** |
| Main idea | The search for the ideal social and political model | The world is led by two opposite forces, **yin and yang**; one must follow the **Dao** (the way) |
| Influence | A form of **meritocracy** at the heart of the Chinese social system to this day; official ideology from Emperor Wu to 1912 | Still the largest religion born in China; includes ancestor worship |

Both belong to the late Zhou period, when central power weakened and local lords fought: paradoxically a golden age of philosophy. The course places them in the **Warring States period**; strictly speaking, Confucius (551–479 BC) lived just before it.

**Checklist (1.5 pt):** founders (0.5); main ideas (0.5); influence on society (0.5).

@part 3 | Essay | Part 3 – Essay | 8 | 45
@q 3.1 | 8
"From the Silk Road to the Korean Wave, art and culture have always been instruments of trade and power in Asia." Discuss, using examples from at least two countries.
@c
**Model plan:**

**Introduction.** Define the terms: art and culture as works (bronze, porcelain, prints) and practices (religions, opera, pop culture); trade and power as economic exchanges, political legitimacy and soft power. Problem: is culture only a product of power and trade, or also a tool that serves them? Announce the plan.

**I. Culture as a tool of political legitimacy**

- The **Mandate of Heaven** (Zhou) justifies power until the 20th century.
- The **First Emperor** (Qin, 221 BC) unifies writing to rule and trade; the **Terracotta Army** shows his quest for immortality.
- **Confucianism** becomes the official ideology under Emperor Wu (Han) and founds a meritocratic state.
- In Japan, the **Yamato** imperial house, the oldest imperial line still reigning, gains recognition from China, which brings techniques and ideas; under the Heian, the court collects works of art.

**II. Culture as a driver of trade and exchange**

- The **Silk Road** under the Han: the first globalisation centred on China; Buddhism enters China through it.
- **Blue-and-white porcelain**, appearing under the Yuan and a worldwide success under the Ming, exported to Europe.
- But trade can also weaken a power: the Ming's **dependence on silver**, then Qing isolationism and the **Opium Wars**.
- In Japan, the **paradox of Edo**: isolation boosts local art (ukiyo-e) thanks to merchants.

**III. Culture as modern soft power**

- Japan after 1945: **manga** culture under American influence and brands such as **Nintendo**.
- South Korea: the **Hallyu**, from K-pop and *Gangnam Style* to *Parasite*.
- China: the return of the **Hanfu**, contemporary art (Yue Minjun), but also the return of control under Xi Jinping.
- Limit: culture can also be destroyed by power, as during the **Cultural Revolution**.

**Conclusion.** Answer the question: yes, culture has served trade and power, but it is more than a tool: it can survive or resist power (Yuan painters who refused to work for the invader, Peking opera still alive). Opening: cultural adaptation in business, such as **McDonald's in India** (no beef or pork).

**Checklist (8 pts):**

- ☐ Introduction: terms defined, problem, plan announced (1.5)
- ☐ Three structured parts that answer the question (2)
- ☐ At least six precise examples with dynasty or date (2)
- ☐ Examples from at least two countries (1)
- ☐ A nuance or limit (culture destroyed or resisting power) (0.5)
- ☐ Conclusion that answers the question, with an opening (1)
