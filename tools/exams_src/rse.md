@intro
**CSR Workshop – Mock final exam – 1h30 – 20 points.** No documents or materials allowed. The exam has three parts: a multiple-choice quiz (one correct answer per question, no negative marking), course questions and a case study.
@method
**Multiple choice:** figures and dates are the most likely questions; learn the CSR timeline and the key figures of the revision sheet.

**Course questions:** definition, then explanation, then an example from the course.

**Case study:** follow the course framework: value chain, diagnosis (Doughnut and ISO 26000), maturity level, legal framework, risks and opportunities, action plan. Every statement must rest on a fact from the case.

@q 1.1 | 0.5
Who coined the expression "social responsibility", and in which year?

A. Freeman, 1984 · B. Bowen, 1953 · C. Rachel Carson, 1962 · D. Brundtland, 1987
@c
**B. Bowen, 1953**, in *Social Responsibilities of the Businessman*. Freeman (1984) is stakeholder theory, Carson (1962) *Silent Spring*, Brundtland (1987) the first definition of sustainable development.
@q 1.2 | 0.5
How many of the 9 planetary boundaries have already been crossed?

A. 3 · B. 5 · C. 7 · D. 9
@c
**C. 7 out of 9.** The boundaries were proposed by Johan Rockström and 28 scientists in 2009.
@q 1.3 | 0.5
What is the CO2 concentration in the atmosphere in 2025?

A. 280 ppm · B. 350 ppm · C. 426 ppm · D. 450 ppm
@c
**C. 426 ppm.** 280 ppm in 1850; 350 ppm is the recommended limit; 450 ppm is the critical threshold (+2 °C, irreversible).
@q 1.4 | 0.5
Who commissioned the Meadows report (*The Limits to Growth*, 1972)?

A. The UN · B. The Club of Rome · C. The OECD · D. The IPCC
@c
**B. The Club of Rome**, in 1970, from MIT researchers. The report is based on the World3 computer model.
@q 1.5 | 0.5
How many needs make up the social foundation of Kate Raworth's Doughnut?

A. 7 · B. 9 · C. 12 · D. 17
@c
**C. 12 needs.** The ecological ceiling corresponds to the 9 planetary boundaries.
@q 1.6 | 0.5
How many goals and targets do the SDGs adopted in 2015 include?

A. 8 goals, 21 targets · B. 17 goals, 169 targets · C. 17 goals, 244 targets · D. 10 goals, 100 targets
@c
**B. 17 goals and 169 targets** (and 244 indicators). The 8 goals are the Millennium Development Goals of 2000.
@q 1.7 | 0.5
Which statement about ISO 26000 is correct?

A. It is mandatory for large companies · B. It is certifiable, like ISO 14001 · C. It is voluntary and not certifiable · D. It only applies to private companies
@c
**C. Voluntary and not certifiable.** Published in 2010 after 5 years of negotiations between 99 countries, it applies to all organisations. They can only be assessed, for example with AFNOR Engagé RSE or Lucie 26000.
@q 1.8 | 0.5
What share of species is threatened with extinction?

A. 5 % · B. 10 % · C. 25 % · D. 50 %
@c
**C. 25 %.** Species are disappearing 100 to 1,000 times faster than the natural rate: this is called the 6th mass extinction.
@q 1.9 | 0.5
How much did the summer 2026 drought cost insurers in France?

A. 500 million euros · B. 1 billion euros · C. More than 5.6 billion euros · D. 42 billion euros
@c
**C. More than 5.6 billion euros**, mainly because of clay shrinkage and swelling (cracked houses). It beats the 2022 record.
@q 1.10 | 0.5
What target did the Kyoto Protocol (1997) set for industrialised countries?

A. Carbon neutrality by 2050 · B. At least −5 % of GHG compared with 1990, over 2008-2012 · C. −55 % of GHG by 2030 · D. Stay below +1.5 °C
@c
**B. At least −5 % of GHG** (5.2 % on average) compared with 1990, over 2008-2012, for 6 gases. Neutrality by 2050 and −55 % by 2030 are the targets of the European Green Deal; 1.5 °C is the Paris Agreement.

**C. 20 %.** This is the course example of a company giving up a product for its regenerative strategy, impACT 2030.
@q 1.12 | 0.5

@part 2 | Course | Part 2 – Course questions | 6 | 25
@q 2.1 | 2
Give the ISO 26000 definition of CSR. Then explain what the CSRD changes compared with the European Commission's 2011 definition.
@c
**Model answer:**

According to ISO 26000 (2010), CSR is the responsibility of an organisation for the impacts of its decisions and activities on society and the environment. It takes the form of ethical and transparent behaviour that contributes to sustainable development, takes stakeholders' expectations into account, complies with the law and international norms, and is integrated throughout the organisation.

In 2011, the European Commission defined CSR as the **voluntary** integration of social and environmental concerns into a company's activities. With the CSRD (2022), CSR becomes partly **mandatory**: large companies must publish a sustainability report following the ESRS standards, carry out a double materiality analysis and have it audited by a third party. The logic shifts from voluntary commitment to imposed transparency: identify, prevent and mitigate negative impacts, then report on them.

**Checklist (2 pts):**

- ☐ ISO 26000 definition with "impacts of decisions and activities" and "society and the environment" (0.5)
- ☐ At least two of the four features of the expected behaviour (0.5)
- ☐ 2011 = voluntary, CSRD = mandatory (0.5)
- ☐ One concrete element of the CSRD: ESRS, double materiality or third-party audit (0.5)
@q 2.2 | 2
Contrast weak sustainability and strong sustainability. Which one underpins the regenerative economy? Illustrate with an example from the course.
@c
**Model answer:**

**Weak sustainability** shows People, Planet and Profit as three overlapping circles on the same level. It assumes that natural capital and human capital are substitutable: technology can replace nature, as long as the total stock of capital does not fall. Its limit is that it ignores thresholds and irreversibility, such as extinctions or tipping points.

**Strong sustainability** nests the circles: profit depends on society, which depends on the planet. Natural capital has irreplaceable functions and must be preserved, in the name of ecological limits and the precautionary principle. In short: no planet, no people; no people, no profit.

The regenerative economy rests on **strong sustainability**: it does not only aim to do less harm, but to have a net positive impact. Example: Interface, with carbon-negative carpet tiles and the Net-Works programme, which recycles abandoned fishing nets.

**Checklist (2 pts):**

- ☐ Weak sustainability: circles on the same level, substitutable capitals (0.5)
- ☐ Strong sustainability: nested circles, irreplaceable natural capital (0.5)
- ☐ The regenerative economy rests on strong sustainability (0.5)
- ☐ One course example: Interface, Mustela or Renault Trucks (0.5)
@q 2.3 | 2
Present Kate Raworth's Doughnut and the three perspectives it opens for companies.
@c
**Model answer:**

The Doughnut (Kate Raworth, Oxfam, 2017) combines two limits. The **ecological ceiling** corresponds to Rockström's 9 planetary boundaries, 7 of which are already crossed. The **social foundation** corresponds to 12 essential needs (food, health, education, water, energy, housing…) below which deprivation begins: for example, 800 million people are undernourished. Between the two lies the ecologically safe and socially just space.

It opens three perspectives: an **integrated view** of the social, the environmental and the economic; a **refocusing**, where social and environmental issues are no longer externalities; and **indicators beyond GDP**, such as tonnes of carbon or the number of people who go hungry. For a company, it is used to map its impacts along its value chain, then to deduce its risks and opportunities, as in the L'Oréal example.

**Checklist (2 pts):**

- ☐ Ceiling = 9 planetary boundaries, foundation = 12 needs (0.5)
- ☐ The safe and just space between them (0.5)
- ☐ The three perspectives (0.5)
- ☐ An application to companies or a figure from the course (0.5)

@part 3 | Case study | Part 3 – Case study: Aventex | 8 | 45
@data
**Aventex** is a French sportswear brand based in Roanne. It has a turnover of **210 M€** and **1,800 employees** in France. It is not listed.

- **Production**: 70 % of its garments are made in Bangladesh and Vietnam by 40 tier-1 suppliers. Aventex does not know its tier-2 and tier-3 suppliers.
- **Materials**: 85 % virgin polyester, made from oil.
- **Carbon**: scopes 1 and 2 are published and have fallen by 12 % in 3 years. Scope 3 is not measured.
- **Governance**: CSR reports to the communications department. The board has 2 women out of 9 members. No variable pay is linked to CSR criteria.
- **Employees**: staff turnover of 9 % (15 % in the sector), gender equality index of 88/100, but workplace accidents are rising in the logistics warehouse.
- **Suppliers**: average payment period of 70 days.
- **Consumers**: the "Eco-responsible collection" campaign is challenged by an NGO, because the collection contains only 8 % recycled materials.
- **Unsold goods**: 300,000 items were destroyed every year until 2021. They are now donated to charities.
- **Local communities**: partnerships with local sports clubs and 1 paid volunteering day per employee per year.

The new CEO wants to move Aventex from a defensive CSR to a strategic CSR.
@q 3.1 | 1.5
Describe Aventex's value chain and identify one impact on the planetary boundaries and one impact on the social foundation of the Doughnut.
@c
**Value chain:** purchase of materials (virgin polyester) → manufacturing by suppliers in Bangladesh and Vietnam → transport to France → logistics warehouse in Roanne → distribution and sales → use (washing) → end of life (unsold goods, used garments).

**Impacts (expected examples):**

- **Planetary boundaries**: virgin polyester made from oil weighs on **climate change**, and microplastics released during washing on **novel entities**.
- **Social foundation**: manufacturing in high-risk countries, with no visibility on tiers 2 and 3, threatens the need for **income and decent work** (the precedent of Rana Plaza, 2013).

**Checklist (1.5 pts):** value chain from upstream to end of life (0.5); one impact on a named planetary boundary (0.5); one impact on a named social need (0.5).
@q 3.2 | 2.5
Carry out the ISO 26000 diagnosis of Aventex as a maturity table, for five core subjects of your choice: a rating (Insufficient, Intermediate, Advanced), a summary and the evidence.
@c
| Core subject | Rating | Summary and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organisational governance | Insufficient | CSR reports to communications, not to the CEO; 2 women out of 9 on the board; no variable pay linked to CSR |
| Human rights | Insufficient | 70 % of production in high-risk countries; no visibility on tiers 2 and 3 |
| Labour practices | Intermediate | Strengths: 9 % turnover vs 15 % in the sector, equality index of 88/100. Weakness: rising accidents in the warehouse |
| The environment | Insufficient | Scopes 1 and 2 down 12 %, but scope 3 not measured although it often exceeds 80 % of the total; 85 % virgin polyester |
| Fair operating practices | Insufficient | 70-day payment period, beyond the legal limit |
| Consumer issues | Insufficient | "Eco-responsible collection" with 8 % recycled content: greenwashing risk, already challenged |
| Community involvement | Intermediate | Local sports partnerships and 1 volunteering day; no local hiring policy yet |

**Checklist (2.5 pts):** 0.5 per core subject, provided the rating is backed by a fact from the case. A rating without evidence earns nothing.
@q 3.3 | 1
What is Aventex's CSR maturity level? Justify.
@c
Aventex is at the **defensive** level, with a few **corrective** elements.

- **Defensive**: CSR reports to communications and serves the brand image (challenged campaign), and unsold goods are donated just as the AGEC law bans destroying them.
- **Corrective**: the 12 % cut in scopes 1 and 2 is about optimising energy costs.
- **Not strategic**: CSR is integrated neither into the strategy nor into the supply chain (scope 3, tiers 2 and 3).

**Checklist (1 pt):** the right level (0.5); two pieces of evidence from the case (0.5).
@q 3.4 | 1
Which legal framework applies to Aventex? Pay attention to thresholds.
@c
- **CSRD**: Aventex is a large company (210 M€, 1,800 employees), so in principle it is covered, but the EU is currently narrowing the scope and delaying the timeline. It should prepare its double materiality analysis and scope 3.
- **Duty of vigilance (French law, 2017)**: **does not apply** legally, because the threshold is 5,000 employees in France. This is the trap. The reputational risk on suppliers is still major, which justifies applying the approach voluntarily.
- **AGEC law**: ban on destroying unsold goods, environmental labelling.
- **REACH**: chemical substances in textiles.
- **PACTE law**: all companies must take social and environmental issues into account; a *raison d'être* or the *société à mission* status remain voluntary.

**Checklist (1 pt):** CSRD and AGEC mentioned (0.5); duty of vigilance correctly ruled out with the threshold (0.5).
@q 3.5 | 2
Propose an action plan of 3 concrete, measurable and realistic actions, following the avoid, reduce, offset, regenerate sequence. Use a best-in-class benchmark.
@c
**Model answer:**

1. **Avoid and reduce on materials**: reach 50 % GRS-certified recycled polyester within 2 years, and run a life-cycle assessment to eco-design the range, since 80 % of a product's impact is decided at the design stage. *Indicator: share of recycled or bio-based materials.*
2. **Reduce supplier risk**: map tier-2 and tier-3 suppliers within 18 months, audit them, then publish the list, as the course suggests in the Shein example. Bring payment periods back within the legal limit. *Indicators: % of suppliers audited, average payment period.*
3. **Reduce by closing the loop at end of life**: collect used garments in stores to recycle them into new fibres, and offer second-hand products. *Indicator: tonnes collected and recycled.*

**Governance to deliver the plan**: make CSR report to the CEO and link part of executives' variable pay to these three indicators. **Benchmark**: Patagonia (organic cotton, recycled polyester); Interface for the closed-loop logic.

**Checklist (2 pts):** three actions that address the weaknesses of the diagnosis (0.5); each with a figure-based indicator and a deadline (0.5); the avoid-reduce-offset-regenerate sequence respected (0.5); a named benchmark (0.5).
